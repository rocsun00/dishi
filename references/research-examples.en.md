# Dishi research examples: read the sources before forming advice

[中文 / Chinese counterpart](research-examples.zh-CN.md)

These two cases are **editorial demonstrations of research and responses**, written after actually running the local `knowledge/kb.py search` and `show` commands during this edit. They are not host-runtime tests, verified accounts of users' lives, expert review, or evidence that the advice works. Searches covered local documents marked `ready` in the catalog. No participants were interviewed; the research does not establish family relationships, a manager's intentions, or a child's psychological state.

Commands ran from the skill root. Each query below actually ran as `python3 knowledge/kb.py search 'query' --limit 6 --json`, with `--book` added where shown. Tables list representative candidates, not complete results. Each passage in the verification lists was then read with `python3 knowledge/kb.py show ID`, including its complete `text`, AI interpretation, cautions, source, and speaker or document identity. The record exposes searches, evidence choices, and responses for review; a host need not reproduce every research note. A lexical match, ranking, or empty result cannot by itself establish whether an idea exists in the corpus.

Research included the [book boundaries](guide.zh-CN.md), [person-specific source guidance](人物原典使用说明.md), the *Analects* [collation notes](../knowledge/lunyu/对读校记.md), and the Wang Yangming collection's [quality notes](../knowledge/wangyangming/质量说明.md). Chinese quotations preserve the local source's characters. Passage IDs are project locators, not universal numbering across editions. **All English renderings of Chinese source text below are our own translations**, not a separately edited English edition. Modern judgments and actions are limited applications developed for these examples.

## 1. A civil servant who was not invited to speak at a meeting

### Question and research entry points

> I'm a civil servant and have just completed probation. At a meeting I wasn't invited to speak, but a colleague said to be the manager's nephew was. I also heard that someone called me “bad at handling people.” What should I do?

The stated facts are the user's employment, completion of probation, and lack of a speaking turn at this meeting. The family relationship, exact criticism, and its speaker remain unverified. Being invited to speak could reflect reporting duties, turn-taking, or preferential treatment. Research must consider the experience of being overlooked, information quality, duties and evaluation standards, opportunities to contribute, fairness, and action costs. It cannot begin by declaring envy, exclusion, or poor social skills.

### Exclusions, comparison, and decision

WYM-01-001 warns about reports, but is Xu Ai's introduction praising his teacher; it cannot be quoted as Wang Yangming's own speech. “Examining Reports” supplies the clearer verification mechanism here. ZGL-01-005 is useful for **correcting the role analogy**, not for establishing exclusion or predicting a reversal of fortune. Quoting only *Han Feizi* 002 while ignoring 006 would wrongly turn attention to duties into a general demand for silence; that reading is rejected.

The materials support checking reports, examining concrete duties and conduct, and refusing to assess fairness solely through relationships or good intentions. Different conditions imply different responses. If the speaking turn reflects routine allocation of duties, clarifying preparation may suffice. Verifiable performance feedback calls for specific improvement. Repeated different treatment under comparable duties would turn attention more directly to organizational fairness. A public challenge can express a position but may amplify misunderstanding before wording and standards are clear. Continued silence avoids immediate friction but may leave expectations unresolved. The present choice is one work-focused conversation. It may feel uncomfortable and may yield no clear answer; its value is helping distinguish these possibilities, not guaranteeing an opportunity.

### Example response

My recommendation is to seek clear duties, evaluation standards, and a way to contribute before deciding whether to pursue a fairness concern. Being overlooked can hurt, but a dispute about favoritism now would rest on unclear information and costs. In *The Art of War*, knowing both sides follows the conditions for deciding whether to fight (SZ-03-007 and 008). The limited application here is to establish who assigns speaking turns, what division of work they follow, and how your actual work enters evaluation.

The Zhuge Liang comparison matters precisely because the roles differ. In *Records of the Three Kingdoms*, Liu Bei already supports him; Guan Yu and Zhang Fei are displeased (ZGL-01-005). You do not yet know whether your immediate supervisor values your contribution, so “ignore others and work quietly” is an inadequate inference. The later biography specifies Liang's administrative and supply responsibilities (ZGL-03-001). Similarly, seek something concrete to own and a working channel through which its results can be seen.

Ask your supervisor about the next assignment: “For meetings like this, which preparation should I take responsibility for, and which part should I report on? What is the main part of my work that needs improvement? Could you give me an example?” This checks duties and performance. In “Establishing Standards,” Han Fei discusses roles and results but also explicitly criticizes “知而弗言” — “knowing but failing to speak” (HF-43-002 and 006). Attention to duty cannot become a blanket instruction to remain silent. Useful work information can be raised briefly through an appropriate channel; a speaking turn at the meeting is a separate expectation to clarify.

Then branch according to the answer. If the supervisor identifies a specific weakness, improve one deliverable and confirm the preparation or report you should own next. If speaking was allocated according to other duties, each turn need not become a test of status. If expectations remain unclear while comparable responsibilities repeatedly attract different standards, retain ordinary assignment, delivery, and feedback records; examine the basis of evaluation and, if needed, consider an appropriate organizational channel. The first route costs the discomfort of speaking up and possibly hearing criticism. The latter needs firmer evidence and may increase relational strain; one meeting does not yet justify jumping to it.

Trace the reported criticism to its wording, speaker, and incident before answering it. The well-digging story in “Examining Reports” restores meaning by asking the people involved (LSCQ-136-002). If the issue is unclear material or slow handovers, improve that work. If there are only vague hints, keep asking about concrete expectations rather than assuming gifts or flattery are required. Apply the same distinction to kinship: appointments should be judged by ability and consistent standards. The recommendation of a son in “Removing Partiality” cannot establish this colleague's incompetence, and the First Memorial's objection to partiality cannot replace an investigation (LSCQ-05-004; ZGL-05-003).

The uncomfortable possibility is that your communication needs improvement, that the treatment is unfair, or that both are true. Endurance should not hide the former, and self-blame should not excuse the latter. Use the next concrete assignment to clarify expectations, take responsibility, and obtain feedback. Change course according to whether the evidence points to a skill gap, different duties, or repeated inconsistency.

## 2. A girl in her first year of high school who lacks enthusiasm for study and likes her phone

### Question and research entry points

> My child is a girl in her first year of high school. She isn't enthusiastic about studying and loves using her phone. What should I do?

This example assumes a parent is asking. If the speaker is the student or a teacher, the person addressed and their authority change. We do not yet know usage time, its effect on study, difficult subjects, when the change began, or the child's own aims. Gender and school year cannot establish laziness, addiction, defiance, or moral failure. Research needs to consider willingness to learn, adverse effects of pressure, tasks and environment, active thinking, and family relationships. A phone-confiscation plan followed by a quotation about resolve would skip that work.

| Environment, accumulation, and lexical noise | `学习 积累 环境` | XZ-08-008 suggests long-term habit. LSCQ-41-003 matches “learning” but belongs to a seasonal calendar, not a method for this problem. |
| Learner initiative | `愤 悱 教` | LY-07-008 helps distinguish prompting from supplying answers; check whether it warrants abandoning an unmotivated learner. |

### Exclusions, comparison, and decision

`show` confirms that LSCQ-41-003 is a seasonal-calendar passage in “Late Autumn,” covering artisans resting and instruction in wind music. A lexical match does not make it evidence about phones or study motivation. ZGL-15-002 remained a search candidate and was not verified with `show` in this research; its familiar quotation is therefore not used. LY-07-008 is retained as a condition for active thinking, not an independent argument to stop teaching. Wang's criticism in 066 also cannot establish that these parents use corporal punishment or that pressure alone explains the phone use.

These materials redirect attention from a verdict about the child's character toward teaching methods, available support, active attempts, and relationship costs. They do not establish one shared cause. Wang opposes crushing pressure while retaining standards. Xunzi stresses aids and accumulation. The *Analects* requires the learner to think: comfort cannot cancel challenge. Mencius warns that correction within a family can turn into mutual injury. Together they support first involving the child in identifying one concrete difficulty, then trying an achievable task with feedback while negotiating phone boundaries. Confiscation cannot repair missing understanding if the task is too difficult; a discussion of interests is insufficient if phone use repeatedly displaces agreed study time. Confiscation may reduce use immediately but carries costs of conflict and concealment. Unlimited use preserves freedom but may let the problem continue. A limited, clear arrangement that can be revisited helps distinguish the conditions, while still requiring observation of actual results.

### Example response

I would replace “push harder and reduce phone use” with an inquiry into three possible chains: whether difficulty is making her avoid study, whether the tasks and available conditions make starting hard, and whether parental correction has become mutual conflict. The phone may be part of these patterns without being their sole cause.

Wang Yangming's guidance on teaching the young describes a possible adverse chain: relentless demands and constraint can make children see school and teachers as things to escape, leading to concealment and avoidance (WYM-02-064–066). If study conversations already become arguments and she mainly uses the phone to escape them, more pressure may deepen avoidance. Change the teaching approach first: stop repeatedly interrogating her about marks, let her identify the subject she finds hardest to enter and a specific obstacle, then arrange help for that point. Reducing pressure costs the parent the immediate reassurance of obedience; it need not remove expectations.

Use Xunzi's account of making good use of aids to investigate another chain (XZ-01-001 and 002). Is willingness missing, or are useful help and steady practice missing? If she is willing to try but cannot understand the work, start with a problem that reveals where she is stuck, address that gap, and let her independently attempt a similar task. If she can do the work but the phone repeatedly pulls her away, focus on conditions and timing: agree where it goes during study, when it can be used, and what the parent must do to support the arrangement. The former needs teaching support; the latter changes conditions of use. Confiscation cannot supply missing understanding, while encouragement alone cannot prevent repeated interruption.

Her own thinking remains essential. The *Analects* says “不憤不啟；不悱不發” — “Do not open the way before the learner struggles to understand, or give expression before the learner struggles to speak” (LY-07-008). A limited application is to let her try, articulate the difficulty, and then receive a useful prompt, rather than having you think through every step. It is not a reason to withhold teaching for lack of initiative. Set the task within her present reach and increase it with progress. Wang's “隨人分限所及” — “within what the person can presently reach” — helps control excessive demands (WYM-03-025). A task she can start, complete, and obtain feedback on reveals more about the obstacle than an immediate demand to reverse every subject.

If correction invariably turns into anger, separate the teaching roles for a while. Mencius describes correction harming the parent-child relationship and proposes instruction by others (MJ-07-018). A limited modern application is for a teacher she trusts to help with a specific subject, while the parent relinquishes some monitoring of every subject and preserves support and shared agreements. This requires finding suitable help, sharing information, and accepting that her way of learning may differ from the parent's preferred method.

First hear which subject is hardest and what she mainly does on the phone, then choose the relevant route. Revisit whether she can start, whether the obstacle is easing, whether the phone still displaces agreed study time, and whether arguments intensify. Adjust support, difficulty, or boundaries accordingly, rather than measuring only obedience. Her explanation is still missing information: no classical story can provide it, and no single arrangement is guaranteed to work at once.

## Scope of use

The depth of these two records demonstrates a reviewable method, not a required response length. Broad inquiry can lead to a short, selective answer; brevity is not a reason to restrict research to one or two schools in advance. Candidates not used, or ideas not retrieved, do not establish that other texts have no relevant thought. No passage verifies the present participants' motives or guarantees that communication or study arrangements will work.
